How an order comes together.

  1. 01

    Consult

    Tell us about the brand, the garments, the quantities, and the date you need them. We’ll recommend blanks and print methods that fit the budget.

  2. 02

    Design

    Our designers help refine your artwork or build it with you, then prepare print-ready files and a mockup for approval.

  3. 03

    Sample

    We produce a sample so you can check fit, fabric, color, and print placement before committing to the full run.

  4. 04

    Produce

    Once the sample is approved, the order is printed, finished, and inspected piece by piece.

  5. 05

    Deliver

    Orders are folded, tagged if requested, packed, and shipped or ready for pickup.

Print and embroidery, explained.

Not sure which to pick? Here’s how each method works and what it’s best at. We’ll recommend one with your quote.

Direct-to-film

DTF

Your design is printed onto a special film, coated with an adhesive powder, then heat-pressed onto the garment, where it bonds as a thin, flexible layer.

Best for
Detailed, full-color designs and small runs on almost any fabric — cotton, polyester, or blends.
Good to know
Works on light and dark garments alike, and the print stays bright.

Direct-to-garment

DTG

A specialized printer sprays water-based ink straight onto the shirt, like an inkjet printer for fabric. The ink soaks into the fibers instead of sitting on top.

Best for
Photos, gradients, and many-color artwork on cotton, even for a single piece.
Good to know
Gives the softest feel of any print. Looks best on 100% cotton.

Screen printing

Silk screen

A stencil is made on a fine mesh screen, one for each color. Ink is pushed through the open parts of the mesh with a squeegee, then cured with heat.

Best for
Bold designs with a few solid colors, especially in larger quantities.
Good to know
Each color needs its own screen, so it gets more affordable per piece the more you order. Very durable.

Dye sublimation

Sublimation

The design is printed on transfer paper, then heat-pressed. The heat turns the ink into a gas that bonds into the polyester fibers, so the print becomes part of the fabric.

Best for
All-over prints, jerseys, activewear, and lanyards.
Good to know
Only works on polyester in white or light colors. It never cracks or peels, and you can’t feel it at all.

Machine embroidery

Embroidery

Your logo is converted into a stitch file, then an embroidery machine sews it into the fabric with thread, giving a raised, textured finish.

Best for
Logos on polos, caps, jackets, and beanies — anything that should look premium.
Good to know
Best for simpler designs without tiny text or gradients. Extremely durable and outlasts the garment.
